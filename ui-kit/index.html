UI Kit

Brand, tokens and menu components shared by every Semaver Games title. Gameplay UI is not covered; each game owns its table.

Brand

The platform logo, then each game's own logo. Click a file name to open the SVG.

Semaver Games

Logo rules

The logo is the mark with the wordmark under it: "SEMAVER" in Montserrat 900 and "GAMES" in Montserrat 700, widely spaced, between two gold rules. The lettering is outlined, so the files need no font.

The Semaver Games mark is a tea glass on a red and white striped saucer, with a spade on the glass and steam rising from inside it.

Rules:

  • Use the stacked logo at 120px wide or more; below that the word "GAMES" gets too small, so use the mark alone.
  • Use the full mark at 64px tall or more; below that use the small version.
  • Keep clear space around it equal to the width of the saucer's rim stripe band on every side.
  • Don't recolor it, stretch it, add a shadow or outline, or place it on photos or busy patterns.
  • Don't crop the steam or the saucer.
  • Don't retype the wordmark in another font, weight or spacing, or rearrange the mark and the words.
  • All files are multi-color; none is a single-ink mark, so none can be tinted with CSS.

Game logos

Game logo pattern

Every game has its own logo, built on one pattern so the games read as a family under Semaver Games.

The pattern:

  • The red plaque with pointed ends and a bowed top and bottom, taken from the Yanık Masası build: three-stop red gradient, 1.5px gold edge, faint inner line, gloss on the top half, dark lip underneath.
  • The game's name in Montserrat 900 capitals, cream with a dark red shadow, on one or two lines, centered on the plaque. The lettering is outlined.
  • The game's emblem on both sides, mirrored and tilted outward about 9 degrees, overlapping the ends of the plaque.

Actions

Button

The one button for every menu action, in five meanings that never swap.

Usage notes

The one button for every menu action, in five meanings that never swap.

ClassMeaningExamples
sg-btn (primary, red)Moves the player forward. One per view.Oyna, Satın Al, Ödülü Al, Giriş Yap
sg-btn--confirm (green)Acknowledges or shows a positive state.Tamam, Takılı
sg-btn--neutralSecondary action or opening a reference screen.Kuralları Aç, Öğreticiyi Aç
sg-btn--ghostNavigation on the felt.Geri
sg-btn--dangerDestructive. Always followed by a confirm modal.Hesabı Sil

Every filled button has the same finish: a three-stop vertical gradient, a 2px trim-gold border and a lip in the color's darkest shade. Red reads primary-top, primary-mid, primary-bottom and primary-lip; green and grey-brown have the same four stops under confirm- and neutral-. A disabled button switches to the locked- stops.

The faceted button, sg-btn sg-btn--hex, is that same finish with pointed ends. Use it where the player enters a game or a destination from the lobby: the button on a GameCard ("Oyna", "Masa Kur", "Giriş Yap") and the PlayBanner. Everywhere else (modals, shop, settings) use the rounded button. The pointed outline is drawn by components/bundle.js, which finds every sg-btn--hex on the page; call Semaver.init() after adding buttons later.

Sizes: sg-btn--sm (36px), default (44px), sg-btn--lg (56px). sg-btn--block fills its container.

You provide: the label, in Turkish, as a verb or verb phrase of one to three words. Write it in normal case; CSS uppercases it, and the page must carry lang="tr" so i becomes İ.

Do:

  • Keep the default height on phones; 44px is the minimum touch target.
  • Use disabled for locked items and say why in the label ("5. Seviye").

Don't:

  • Use the faceted button inside modals, shop items or settings rows, or put two of them next to each other outside a row of game cards.
  • Put two primary buttons side by side. Pair primary with neutral or ghost.
  • Use green for buying or playing, or red for on/off state. A setting that turns on and off is a Switch.

PlayBanner

The single largest control in a game's lobby: one tap seats the player at a table.

Usage notes

The single largest control in a game's lobby: one tap seats the player at a table.

A wide faceted button: the same gradient, 2px gold border and lip as every other button, with pointed ends and a soft amber glow (play-glow). Full width of the screen gutter, at least 104px tall. The live dot before the player count uses status-online. The outline is drawn by components/bundle.js.

You provide: the label ("Hemen Oyna") and the live online count for the meta line. Hide the meta line when the count is unknown; don't show zero.

One per lobby, placed where the thumb reaches: directly above the bottom nav on phones. Decorative art (flames, cards) may sit at the two ends on wide screens only and must never cover the label.

Switch

An on/off control for settings, replacing the Açık / Kapalı buttons.

Usage notes

An on/off control for settings, replacing the Açık / Kapalı buttons.

A button with role="switch" and aria-checked. Green track means on, neutral means off; the knob position carries the state too, so it does not rely on color alone.

You provide: an aria-label or a visible label in a SettingsRow, and a click handler that flips aria-checked and saves the setting immediately. No separate save step.

Use it for: sound, chat sound, vibration, notifications.

Don't use it for actions that open something or change more than one value (theme picker, sign in). Those are a Button.

Tabs

A single row of category tabs, used at the top of the shop.

Usage notes

A single row of category tabs, used at the top of the shop.

The selected tab is red with a gold underline; the rest are neutral. On a phone the row scrolls sideways inside itself and never wraps to a second line.

You provide: two to eight short labels (one or two words) and which one is selected (aria-selected="true").

Don't use tabs for steps in a flow or for filters that can combine. Keep the selected tab in the URL as a plain name, for example /shop?tab=deste.

Usage notes

The fixed bar at the bottom of the lobby with the secondary destinations.

Guidelines

The full rules: what colors mean, type, writing, shape and behavior.

The shared menu and shell UI for every Semaver Games title: lobby, shop, settings, rewards, modals. It does not cover gameplay (the table, cards in hand, turn indicators); each game owns that.

Brand

  • Semaver Games is the platform; each game is its own title under it. The platform logo is the tea glass on a striped saucer above the "SEMAVER GAMES" wordmark (Logos assets). Each game has its own logo on the shared red plaque (Game Logos assets).
  • Inside a game, the game's logo leads: lobby header, loading screen, share images. The Semaver Games mark appears small, on the splash screen and in settings or the footer.
  • On the platform's own surfaces (semavergames.com, the store publisher page, the game picker) the Semaver Games logo leads and game logos are the items being chosen.
  • Use the logo files as they are. Never retype a wordmark or redraw an emblem.

How to use it

  • Load tokens.css, then components.css, then components.js. Components are plain HTML with sg- classes; the script only draws the faceted button shape.
  • Read a component's README before using it. Each one says what you provide and what its colors mean.
  • Set lang="tr" on the page. Labels are uppercased by CSS, and Turkish needs it to turn i into İ.
  • Build for a phone held upright first (360px wide, 16px gutter from space-4), then let it widen. Nothing may run off the side of the screen.

What the colors mean

The look is a card table: bg-table felt, surface-panel cream panels, trim-gold edges. On top of that, button color is a promise and never changes:

ColorTokenMeaning
Redprimary- gradientMoves the player forward: play, buy, claim, sign in. One per view.
Greenconfirm- gradientA positive state or an acknowledgement: on, equipped, OK.
Grey-brownneutral- gradientSecondary actions, opening a reference screen, the off state.
Outline red on creamtext-heading borderDestructive, always confirmed in a modal.
  • Text on the felt and on filled buttons is text-on-dark. Text on cream is text-on-panel, with text-heading for titles and text-on-panel-muted for hints.
  • trim-gold is for borders only. For gold text on dark use trim-gold-light. Never set gold text on cream.
  • coin is the coin face, not a text color. status-online is the live dot next to a player count.
  • Keyboard focus is a 3px focus-ring outline with a 2px offset on every control.

Type

One family, Montserrat, in three weights (600, 700, 900), loaded from Google Fonts.

  • display, title, heading and label are uppercase with a little letter spacing. Use them for names of things and for buttons.
  • body and caption are sentence case. Anything the player reads as a sentence goes here.
  • numeric is for coin amounts and prices, with tabular figures.

Writing

  • Turkish, informal second person singular: "Hediyen seni bekliyor", "İlerlemeni kaydet".
  • Buttons are a verb or verb phrase of one to three words: "Oyna", "Satın Al", "Ödülü Al", "Masa Kur".
  • Hints are one short sentence without a full stop: "Kart, sıra ve bitiş sesleri".
  • Numbers use the Turkish thousands dot: 1.500, 12.400. Coins are called "para" and never carry a money symbol.
  • Labels are never truncated. If a label does not fit, shorten the word, not the box.

Shape and depth

  • Corners: radius-md on buttons and tiles, radius-lg on panels and modals, radius-xl on lobby cards, radius-pill on pills, switches and badges, radius-sm on tabs and chips. No other corner sizes.
  • Every filled button has one finish: a three-stop vertical gradient (primary-, confirm- or neutral- with -top, -mid, -bottom), a 2px trim-gold border, and a 4px lip in the matching -lip color. It moves down 2px when pressed. No gloss, no inner lines.
  • The faceted button (sg-btn--hex) and the play banner are that same finish with pointed ends. The shape alone marks entering a game from the lobby, and it is not used anywhere else.
  • Panels resting on the felt use shadow-card; anything floating above it uses shadow-panel.
  • The diamond rule (sg-rule) sits under a panel or modal title, once per surface.

Behavior rules

  • One unprompted modal per session start at most. Waiting rewards show as a badge.
  • Every modal can be closed with its close button, Escape, or a tap outside.
  • A setting that is on or off is a Switch. A button never doubles as a state display.
  • The bottom nav holds at most five items.
  • Touch targets are at least 44px tall; small buttons (36px) are only for rows inside a panel.

Themes

yanik is the first and default theme. A new game adds a theme that overrides only the primary- stops and the action-primary family and, if it wants its own table, bg-table and bg-table-deep. Panels, text, gold, green and neutral stay the same across games so the menus feel like one platform.

Iconography and art

  • The bottom nav uses 24px line icons, 2px stroke, round caps, in the label's color.
  • The suit glyphs in GameCard and the plain card backs in ShopItem are placeholders. Each game supplies its own illustrations for those slots.
  • The logo is the tea glass mark above the "SEMAVER GAMES" wordmark, in the Logos assets (logo-stacked-on-dark.svg, logo-stacked-on-light.svg). Use the mark on its own (mark-on-dark.svg, mark-on-light.svg) where the name is already present, and the small versions under 64px. Never retype the wordmark; use the files.
  • No emoji in the UI.

Intentional changes from the first Yanık build

  • Switch replaces the Açık / Kapalı buttons in settings.
  • Green and red buttons now have one meaning each, as in the table above.
  • Seven corner sizes became five.
  • Playfair Display and Oswald are dropped; only Montserrat is loaded.
  • The bottom nav went from six items to five so labels fit on a phone.
  • Layout is phone-upright first.
